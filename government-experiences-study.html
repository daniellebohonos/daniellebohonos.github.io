Participant Information

Government Experiences Study

Thank you for your willingness to consider participating in the Government Experiences Study. This page explains what the study is about, what taking part involves, and how your information is protected.

Purpose of the study

The purpose of this study is to better understand individuals' perspectives regarding their experiences with government services. More specifically, the study seeks to understand how individuals interact with government institutions in their daily lives, what their day-to-day experiences with social services look like, and how these experiences shape their broader perceptions of government. This research is being conducted by me, Danielle Bohonos, a PhD candidate in the Department of Political Science at the University of Toronto, as part of my doctoral research.

Who can participate, and what is involved?

I am inviting adult residents (18 years or older) of the communities I visit to take part. Participation involves an informal conversation with me about your experiences with government and social services, typically lasting between 15 and 45 minutes, in a public or community setting (such as a community event, café, or market). There is no preparation required, and you may share as much or as little as you wish.

What information will be collected?

I will collect information on the demographic identities (for example, gender, age group, and racial identity) of some participants only where such information arises naturally in conversation. I will never disclose personal information if it would risk making a participant personally identifiable.

Are there risks or benefits if I participate?

There are no known risks to participating in this study, nor are there any direct benefits to you for participating. I hope that this research will improve our understanding of how government serves individuals.

Is participation voluntary?

Yes. Your participation is completely voluntary. You may refuse to participate, decline to answer any question, or withdraw at any time, without any negative consequences. By participating, you do not waive any of your legal rights.

What happens to the information I provide?

I am deeply committed to keeping your data confidential. My field notes will be securely stored in a password-protected and encrypted folder, and only one individual (Danielle Bohonos) will ever have access to this information.

You are free to withdraw from the study until November 1, 2027. To withdraw, please contact me at danielle.bohonos@mail.utoronto.ca to communicate that you wish to withdraw, along with a reminder of where and when we interacted, so that I can remove our interaction from my field notes; because my notes do not record participants' names, I will rely on these details to identify the relevant notes.

The information that you provide will be used in academic research on citizens' experiences with government and social services, and may appear in academic publications and presentations. To protect confidentiality and privacy, your information will be protected in the following ways:

  • I will never record your personal name, even in my hand-written field notes.
  • I will never disclose the location of our interaction at a level below that of the community.
  • I will never disclose personal information about you if there is any risk that such information could allow another person to identify you. For instance, I would only disclose your gender or age group if our interaction took place in a context involving many others of the same gender or age group.
